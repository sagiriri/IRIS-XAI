IRIS-XAI: A Dual Ground-Truth and Function-Grounded Benchmark for Explainable Computer Vision
Resolving the Perturbation Fallacy: Benchmarking Grad-CAM, IRIS-CAM, Integrated Gradients, SHAP, and LIME Across Four Deep Vision Architectures
Hemant Kumar1, Ramesh Chand Pandey1
1Department of Information Technology, Rajkiya Engineering College, Ambedkar Nagar, U.P., India
hemantmathur9807@gmail.com, rcpandey@recabn.ac.in
Abstract—Post-hoc explainability techniques (XAI) are essential for validating deep neural networks in mission-critical applications. However, contemporary benchmarking suites evaluate explainers almost exclusively through function-grounded perturbation metrics (e.g., Deletion and Insertion AUC). In this paper, we expose the "Ground-Truth vs. Perturbation Fallacy": a model-centric perturbation test can reward an attribution method with an ostensibly superior Deletion AUC even when the explanation predominantly focuses on background distractor clutter. To resolve this blind spot, we present IRIS-XAI, a dual-evaluation benchmarking framework that unites function-grounded perturbation tests with pixel-level ground-truth semantic validation across four convolutional neural network backbones (SimpleCNN, ResNet-18, ResNet-50, and EfficientNet-B0) on CIFAR-10 and FunnyBirds. We evaluate five diverse XAI algorithms: Grad-CAM, Integrated Gradients (Captum), SHAP (GradientExplainer), LIME, and our proposed IRIS-CAM (Adaptive Energy-Gated CAM). Establishing exact 1:1 architectural parity with Skliarov et al. (Nature Scientific Reports, 2025) on ResNet-50, our empirical results reveal that while Integrated Gradients achieves high Deletion AUC, it incurs severe background clutter leakage (20.5%–23.7%), scattering attribution across non-diagnostic textures. In contrast, IRIS-CAM prunes diffuse bilinear halos through dynamic energy thresholding (τ = Percentile70), reducing background clutter leakage to 4.9%–6.1% (a 4× to 5× reduction over Integrated Gradients), running 15× faster than Integrated Gradients and 184× faster than SHAP, and securing Rank 1 overall in Composite Explainability Score across benchmarked architectures.
Keywords—Explainable Artificial Intelligence (XAI), Ground-Truth Evaluation, FunnyBirds Benchmark, IRIS-CAM, Integrated Gradients, Background Clutter Leakage, ResNet-50 Parity.

I. Introduction

Deep convolutional neural networks (CNNs) have established remarkable accuracy in computer vision tasks. Nonetheless, their black-box behavior presents grave reliability concerns in safety-critical sectors, including autonomous transit, biomedical diagnostics, and security surveillance. Explainable Artificial Intelligence (XAI) seeks to mitigate this opacity by producing saliency maps that visualize which spatial regions governed the classifier's inference.

A recent prominent benchmark by Skliarov et al. [1] published in Nature Scientific Reports (2025) evaluated six XAI methods across ResNet-50, VGG-16, and Vision Transformers on CIFAR-10, SVHN, and Imagenette. Their primary empirical conclusion declared gradient-path methods—specifically Integrated Gradients [2] and SmoothGrad—as the premier explainers, citing superior fidelity (Deletion AUC) and stability. However, their benchmarking paradigm is strictly function-grounded: attributions are quantified exclusively by perturbing top-ranked pixels and measuring the drop in model output confidence, lacking any external verification of whether those pixels represent genuine semantic features.

In this work, we reveal the "Ground-Truth vs. Perturbation Fallacy": high-frequency pixel gradients routinely trigger steep confidence collapses because deep neural decision boundaries are hyper-sensitive to localized noise artifacts. Saliency maps with scattered high-frequency gradients can thus achieve exceptional Deletion AUC despite attributing predictions to irrelevant background clutter. To rectify this, we deploy the FunnyBirds benchmark (Hesse et al., ICCV 2023) [3], where synthetic avian images feature precise pixel-level semantic part maps (beak, eye, foot, wing), enabling true ground-truth validation alongside classical perturbation metrics.

Principal Contributions:

1) Dual Ground-Truth & Perturbation Benchmark: We benchmark 4 CNN backbones across CIFAR-10 and FunnyBirds under a standardized protocol, achieving 1:1 architecture parity with Skliarov et al. [1] via ResNet-50.

2) Empirical Clutter Discovery: We prove that Integrated Gradients and SHAP suffer massive background clutter leakage (20.5%–23.7%), while activation-mapping methods localize features with 3–5× less background noise.

3) Proposed IRIS-CAM: We introduce Adaptive Energy-Gated CAM, which prunes diffuse bilinear upsampling halos, achieving Rank 1 in composite explainability (70.6 vs 56.2 for Integrated Gradients) with 15× faster latency.

II. Related Work

Function-Grounded Benchmarking: Frameworks such as Saliency-Bench [4], ExplainTS [5], BEExAI [9], and Skliarov et al. [1] standardize metrics including Deletion/Insertion AUC, sensitivity, and cosine stability. While essential for characterizing internal model consistency, they cannot diagnose whether an explanation points to the correct visual concept.

Ground-Truth Vision Benchmarks: Hesse et al. [3] created FunnyBirds to provide an objective, part-based testbed for visual XAI. Unlike natural datasets with coarse bounding boxes, FunnyBirds contains exact pixel-level part segmentations, allowing precise quantification of part overlap and background clutter leakage.

Attribution Methods: Path-integral algorithms (Integrated Gradients [2]) satisfy mathematical axioms of completeness and implementation invariance but are prone to gradient shattering. Perturbation algorithms (SHAP [6], LIME [7]) compute superpixel attributions but require massive sampling budgets. Activation mapping (Grad-CAM [8]) pools coarse feature maps, delivering fast localization but suffering from diffuse bilinear spread.

IV. Methodology & Proposed IRIS-CAM

Standard Grad-CAM [8] computes neuron importance weights αkc by global-average-pooling feature gradients:

αkc = (1/Z) Σi Σj (∂Yc / ∂Ai,jk),   Mraw = ReLU(Σk αkc Ak)

Upsampling Mraw to input resolution (224×224) via bilinear interpolation creates a diffuse halo that spills into background pixels. We introduce IRIS-CAM (Adaptive Energy-Gated CAM), which prunes sub-threshold background energy using dynamic percentile thresholding:

τ = Percentile70(Mraw),   MIRIS = [Mraw · 𝕀(Mraw ≥ τ)] / max(Mraw)

By adaptively gating out the lowest 70% energy distribution, IRIS-CAM eliminates peripheral leakage while concentrating attribution density on the anatomical core.

Composite Explainability Score: Following standard multi-criteria evaluation, five normalized metrics are averaged into a composite index (0–100): Insertion AUC, (1 - Deletion AUC), Cosine Stability, (1 - Max Sensitivity), and (1 - Normalized Shannon Entropy).

VII. Engineering Challenges & Solutions

Three critical engineering challenges were diagnosed and resolved during framework implementation:

1) Device Migration Side-Effect: Explainers initially forced CPU execution when evaluating batches, silently shifting GPU models to CPU. Resolved by having all explainers dynamically query the model's active torch device.

2) LIME Label Default Override: LIME's top_labels=5 parameter silently overrode explicit target class indices, yielding out-of-bounds exceptions. Resolved by explicitly configuring top_labels=None.

3) SHAP Gradient Resolution: Executing SHAP at full 224×224 resolution triggered severe GPU autograd stalls. Downsampling inputs to 64×64 before computing SHAP coalitions and upsampling the attributions restored high stability with negligible quality loss.

VIII. Discussion & Limitations

Our empirical findings expose the fundamental peril of relying exclusively on perturbation tests. Because deep neural networks are vulnerable to high-frequency pixel perturbations, masking top Integrated Gradients pixels collapses output logits rapidly, earning an impressive Deletion AUC. Yet, as our FunnyBirds ground-truth maps definitively prove, over 20% of those pixels lie in irrelevant background clutter. True interpretability demands both internal model faithfulness and external semantic alignment.

Limitations: Tail segmentations were omitted from FunnyBirds analysis due to geometric camera occlusions. Additionally, SHAP calculations utilized downsampled feature representations to maintain computational feasibility.

IX. Conclusion

IRIS-XAI establishes a dual ground-truth and function-grounded benchmarking platform for explainable computer vision. By evaluating ResNet-50 in 1:1 parity with Skliarov et al. [1] and cross-validating against FunnyBirds part maps (Hesse et al. [3]), we exposed the background clutter vulnerability of Integrated Gradients. Furthermore, our proposed IRIS-CAM delivers superior semantic precision, suppresses clutter leakage by up to 5×, operates in 0.04s, and commands Rank 1 across all benchmarked vision architectures.
