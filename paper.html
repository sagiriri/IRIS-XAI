IRIS-XAI: A Dual Ground-Truth and Function-Grounded Benchmark for Explainable Computer Vision
Empirical Analysis of Perturbation Fallacies: Evaluating Grad-CAM, Energy-Gated Grad-CAM, Integrated Gradients, SHAP, and LIME on CIFAR-10 and FunnyBirds
Hemant Kumar1, Ramesh Chand Pandey1
1Department of Information Technology, Rajkiya Engineering College, Ambedkar Nagar, U.P., India
hemantmathur9807@gmail.com, rcpandey@recabn.ac.in
Abstract—Post-hoc explainable AI (XAI) techniques are crucial for auditing deep neural networks in mission-critical vision applications. However, contemporary benchmarks evaluate explainers almost exclusively through function-grounded perturbation tests (e.g., Deletion and Insertion AUC). In this work, we demonstrate that perturbation tests alone induce an empirical blind spot: an attribution method can achieve top-tier Deletion AUC on a classifier while predominantly scattering its attribution across irrelevant background clutter. To resolve this, we present IRIS-XAI, a unified benchmarking framework combining rigorous function-grounded metrics with pixel-level semantic ground-truth validation across four CNN backbones (SimpleCNN, ResNet-18, ResNet-50, and EfficientNet-B0) on CIFAR-10 and FunnyBirds. We evaluate five representative attribution techniques: Grad-CAM, Integrated Gradients (Captum), SHAP (GradientExplainer), LIME, and an energy-gated ablation variant, Energy-Gated Grad-CAM (EG-GradCAM). Establishing exact 1:1 architectural parity with Skliarov et al. (Nature Scientific Reports, 2025) on ResNet-50, our empirical findings confirm that while Integrated Gradients exhibits strong Deletion AUC, it incurs severe background clutter leakage (20.5%–23.7%) on FunnyBirds part maps, dispersing pixel gradients across non-diagnostic textures. In contrast, activation mapping restricts background clutter to under 7%. Furthermore, by pruning diffuse bilinear upsampling halos via dynamic energy thresholding, EG-GradCAM reduces clutter leakage to 4.8%–6.1% (a 4× reduction over Integrated Gradients), operates in 0.04s (15× faster than Integrated Gradients and 184× faster than SHAP), and secures the highest composite explainability score across benchmarked backbones.
Keywords—Explainable Artificial Intelligence (XAI), Ground-Truth Evaluation, FunnyBirds Benchmark, Integrated Gradients, Energy-Gated Grad-CAM, Background Clutter Leakage, ResNet-50.

I. Introduction

Deep convolutional neural networks (CNNs) have demonstrated exceptional accuracy across diverse visual perception tasks. Nonetheless, their black-box nature poses grave reliability concerns in safety-critical domains, including medical diagnostics, autonomous navigation, and automated industrial inspection. Explainable Artificial Intelligence (XAI) techniques generate saliency maps that highlight which spatial regions influenced the classifier's inference.

A recent comprehensive benchmark by Skliarov et al. [1] published in Nature Scientific Reports (2025) evaluated six XAI methods across ResNet-50, VGG-16, and Vision Transformers on CIFAR-10, SVHN, and Imagenette. Their primary conclusion favored gradient-path methods—specifically Integrated Gradients [2] and SmoothGrad—citing superior fidelity (Deletion AUC) and stability. However, their benchmarking paradigm is strictly function-grounded: attributions are quantified exclusively by masking top-ranked pixels and measuring the drop in model output confidence, lacking any external verification of whether those pixels represent genuine semantic features.

In this work, we reveal the "Ground-Truth vs. Perturbation Fallacy": high-frequency pixel gradients routinely trigger steep confidence collapses because deep neural decision boundaries are hyper-sensitive to localized noise artifacts. Saliency maps with scattered high-frequency gradients can thus achieve exceptional Deletion AUC despite attributing predictions to irrelevant background clutter. To rectify this, we deploy the FunnyBirds benchmark (Hesse et al., ICCV 2023) [3], where synthetic avian images feature precise pixel-level semantic part maps (beak, eye, foot, wing), enabling true ground-truth validation alongside classical perturbation metrics.

Principal Contributions:

1) Dual Ground-Truth & Perturbation Benchmark: We benchmark 4 CNN backbones across CIFAR-10 and FunnyBirds under a standardized protocol, achieving 1:1 architecture parity with Skliarov et al. [1] via ResNet-50.

2) Empirical Clutter Discovery: We prove that Integrated Gradients and SHAP suffer massive background clutter leakage (20.5%–23.7%), while activation-mapping methods localize features with 3–5× less background noise.

3) Principled Ablation (EG-GradCAM): We examine Energy-Gated Grad-CAM, which prunes diffuse bilinear upsampling halos, achieving Rank 1 in composite explainability (70.6 vs 56.2 for Integrated Gradients) with 15× faster latency.

II. Related Work

Function-Grounded Benchmarking: Frameworks such as Saliency-Bench [4], ExplainTS [5], BEExAI [9], and Skliarov et al. [1] standardize metrics including Deletion/Insertion AUC, sensitivity, and cosine stability. While essential for characterizing internal model consistency, they cannot diagnose whether an explanation points to the correct visual concept.

Ground-Truth Vision Benchmarks: Hesse et al. [3] created FunnyBirds to provide an objective, part-based testbed for visual XAI. Unlike natural datasets with coarse bounding boxes, FunnyBirds contains exact pixel-level part segmentations, allowing precise quantification of part overlap and background clutter leakage.

Attribution Methods: Path-integral algorithms (Integrated Gradients [2]) satisfy mathematical axioms of completeness and implementation invariance but are prone to gradient shattering. Perturbation algorithms (SHAP [6], LIME [7]) compute superpixel attributions but require massive sampling budgets. Activation mapping (Grad-CAM [8]) pools coarse feature maps, delivering fast localization but suffering from diffuse bilinear spread.

IV. Methodology & Energy-Gated Grad-CAM

Standard Grad-CAM [8] computes neuron importance weights αkc by global-average-pooling feature gradients:

αkc = (1/Z) Σi Σj (∂Yc / ∂Ai,jk),   Mraw = ReLU(Σk αkc Ak)

Upsampling Mraw to input resolution (224×224) via bilinear interpolation creates a diffuse halo that spills into background regions. We evaluate an energy-gated ablation, EG-GradCAM, which prunes sub-threshold background energy using dynamic percentile thresholding:

τ = Percentile70(Mraw),   Gate = [1 + exp(-(Mraw - τ)/T)]-1,   MEG = Mraw · Gate

By adaptively gating out the lowest 70% energy distribution, EG-GradCAM mitigates bilinear spread while concentrating attribution density on the anatomical core.

Composite Explainability Score: Following standard multi-criteria evaluation, five normalized metrics are averaged into a composite index (0–100): Insertion AUC, (1 - Deletion AUC), Cosine Stability, (1 - Max Sensitivity), and (1 - Normalized Shannon Entropy).

VII. Engineering Challenges & Solutions

Three critical engineering challenges were diagnosed and resolved during framework implementation:

1) Device Migration Side-Effect: Explainers initially forced CPU execution when evaluating batches, silently shifting GPU models to CPU. Resolved by having all explainers dynamically query the model's active torch device.

2) LIME Label Default Override: LIME's top_labels=5 parameter silently overrode explicit target class indices, yielding out-of-bounds exceptions. Resolved by explicitly configuring top_labels=None.

3) SHAP Gradient Resolution: Executing SHAP at full 224×224 resolution triggered severe GPU autograd stalls. Downsampling inputs to 64×64 before computing SHAP coalitions and upsampling the attributions restored high stability with negligible quality loss.

VIII. Discussion & Limitations

Our empirical findings expose the fundamental peril of relying exclusively on perturbation tests. Because deep neural networks are vulnerable to high-frequency pixel perturbations, masking top Integrated Gradients pixels collapses output logits rapidly, earning an impressive Deletion AUC. Yet, as our FunnyBirds ground-truth maps definitively prove, over 20% of those pixels lie in irrelevant background clutter. True interpretability demands both internal model faithfulness and external semantic alignment.

Limitations: Tail segmentations were omitted from FunnyBirds analysis due to geometric camera occlusions. Additionally, SHAP calculations utilized downsampled feature representations to maintain computational feasibility.

IX. Conclusion

IRIS-XAI establishes a dual ground-truth and function-grounded benchmarking platform for explainable computer vision. By evaluating ResNet-50 in 1:1 parity with Skliarov et al. [1] and cross-validating against FunnyBirds part maps (Hesse et al. [3]), we exposed the background clutter vulnerability of Integrated Gradients. Furthermore, our ablation study demonstrates that Energy-Gated Grad-CAM (EG-GradCAM) mitigates bilinear upsampling blur, cuts clutter leakage to 6.1%, operates in 0.04s, and delivers a balanced, noise-free explanation for real-world computer vision systems.
